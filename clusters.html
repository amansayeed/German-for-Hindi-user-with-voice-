⚠️ Letters That Sound Different from English

Click a letter to hear its name; click an example word to hear it in a word. Focus on these first!

Letter English sound German sound Example (click 🔊)
A "ay" (name), "a" (cat), "ah" (father) — varies. Open "ah" — like father. Never "ay". Short [a] in closed syllables. Tag 🔊
Ä No equivalent in English. Like "eh" in air or bed — mouth open. Long or short. Bär 🔊
E "ee" (see), "eh" (bed), "uh" (the) — varies. Pure "eh" — long [eː] or short open [ɛ]. No glide like English. Beet 🔊
G "g" (go) or "j" (gem). Hard "g" at start; at end of syllable → "k"; after e/i/ä/ö/ü often soft "ch" [ç]. König 🔊
H "h" (house) — always pronounced. "h" only at start of syllable; after a vowel = silent (vowel lengthened). gehen 🔊
I "eye" (I), "ee" (see), "ih" (bit) — varies. Long "ee" [iː] or short "i" [ɪ] — pure, no diphthong. Never "eye". Bild 🔊
J "j" [dʒ] (jar, jump). Always "y" [j] like yes — never like English "j". Jahr 🔊
K "k" with a puff of air (key, cat). "k" with no puff — crisp, like "k" in ski. Kind 🔊
Ö No equivalent in English. Say "eh" then round your lips. Like "er" in British "her" but more rounded. schön 🔊
P "p" with a puff of air (pin, pet). "p" with no puff — like "p" in spin (after s). Park 🔊
Q "kw" (queen, quick). Always "kv" — Q only appears as Qu. Say "k" + "v". Quelle 🔊
R "r" (red) — tip of tongue or American "r". Guttural/back of throat [ʁ] (like French); at end of syllable often vocalized like soft "a" [ɐ]. rot 🔊
T "t" with a puff of air (top, ten). "t" with no puff — crisp, like "t" in stop. Tag 🔊
U "uh" (cup), "oo" (put), "yoo" (use) — varies. Pure "oo" [uː] like moon — no "y" sound, lips rounded. gut 🔊
Ü No equivalent in English. Say "ee" (see) with lips rounded (puckered). über 🔊
V "v" (very, love). Usually "f" (Vater, von); in loanwords sometimes "v". Vater 🔊
W "w" (water) — lips rounded. "v" — lips not rounded! Like English v in very. Wasser 🔊
Y "y" (yes) or "i" (myth). In loanwords: like "ü" (Yoga) or "y" (Yacht). Rare in native words. Yoga 🔊
Z "z" [z] (zoo) — voiced. "ts" [ts] — like cats or pizza. Unvoiced, crisp. Zwei 🔊
ß No equivalent (we use "ss"). Sharp "ss" [s] after a long vowel; never voiced. Not "z". Straße 🔊

🔴 Rule 3: Complete Pronunciation Rules Summary

📌 Golden Rules (सुनहरे नियम):

  • W = व (NOT English "w") → Wasser = वासर
  • V = फ (NOT English "v") → Vater = फाटर
  • J = य (NOT English "j") → Jahr = यार
  • Z = त्स (NOT English "z") → Zwei = त्स्वाइ
  • TH = त (NOT English "th") → Theater = तेआटर
  • SCH = श (ALWAYS!) → Schule = शुले
  • ST at start = श्ट (NOT स्ट) → Stadt = श्टाट
  • SP at start = श्प (NOT स्प) → Sport = श्पोर्ट
  • CH = ख/ह्य (NOT च) → ich = इख, Buch = बुख
  • KN: K is NOT silent! → Knie = क्नी
  • R = guttural/uvular र (NOT English rolled R) → Rot = रोट

💡 Memory Tricks (याद रखने की ट्रिक):

  • W and V are SWAPPED! W sounds like English V, V sounds like English F!
  • J is like Y → Think "Yahr" not "Jahr"
  • Z is like TS → Think "TSwei" not "Zwei"
  • TH is just T → Think "Teater" not "Theater"
  • SCH is always SH → Think "Shule" not "School"
  • ST/SP at start = SHT/SHP → Think "Shtadt" not "Stadt"
  • CH is never CH → Think "ikh" not "ich"
  • KN: K is NEVER silent! → Think "K-nie" not "nie"
  • R is guttural/uvular → Think throaty "R" (like French) not English rolled R!

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid (बचने के लिए सामान्य गलतियाँ):

  • ❌ Don't say: "वासर" (like English "water")
  • ✅ Say: "वासर" (W = व, but different from English W sound)
  • ❌ Don't say: "वाटर" (like English "father")
  • ✅ Say: "फाटर" (V = फ, not व!)
  • ❌ Don't say: "जार" (like English "jar")
  • ✅ Say: "यार" (J = य, not ज!)
  • ❌ Don't say: "स्कूल" (like English "school")
  • ✅ Say: "शुले" (SCH = श, always!)
  • ❌ Don't say: "स्टाट" (like English "start")
  • ✅ Say: "श्टाट" (ST at start = श्ट!)
  • ❌ Don't say: "रोट" (English rolled R)
  • ✅ Say: "रोट" (R = guttural/uvular र, throaty sound like French!)

🎯 Practice Tips (अभ्यास युक्तियाँ):

  • Click 🔊 on each word to hear the correct pronunciation!
  • Practice saying these words OUT LOUD every day!
  • Focus on ONE letter/cluster at a time - don't try to learn all at once!
  • Write down words that look English but sound different!
  • Test yourself: Can you pronounce "Wasser", "Vater", "Jahr" correctly?
  • Remember: German pronunciation is CONSISTENT - once you learn the rules, you can read any word!

CH - The Tricky One! 🎯

🔊 Pronunciation Rule:
- After front vowels (e, i, ä, ö, ü) → soft [ç] (like ह in "ह्य") e.g., ich.
- After back vowels (a, o, u) → hard [χ] (guttural, like Scottish 'loch').
- Tip: Front vowels = soft "ह्य" sound, Back vowels = throaty "ख" sound!
💡 Memory Tip: CH has TWO sounds! Front vowels (e, i, ä, ö, ü) = soft like "ich" (इख), "Licht" (लिख्ट), "sprechen" (श्प्रेखेन). Back vowels (a, o, u) = hard like "Buch" (बुख), "auch" (औख), "noch" (नोख). Remember: "ich" is soft, "Buch" is hard!
