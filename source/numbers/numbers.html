🔢 German Numbers (A1)

0–100, big numbers, and ordinals. Correct German with English and Hindi. Pronunciation hints included. Ideal for Goethe A1.

📐 How German numbers work

Pattern for 21–99: Say the ones first, then und, then the ten. Example: 21 = ein + und + zwanzig → einundzwanzig (one-and-twenty). Same for 35: fünfunddreißig (five-and-thirty).

Key parts: -zehn = teen (13–19: dreizehn, vierzehn …); -zig = ty (zwanzig, dreißig, vierzig …). Note: 30 = dreißig (ß, not z).

  • 0–12: Learn by heart (null, eins, zwei … elf, zwölf).
  • 13–19: digit + zehn (e.g. siebzehn from sieben).
  • 20, 30 … 90: zwanzig, dreißig, vierzig, fünfzig, sechzig, siebzig, achtzig, neunzig.
  • 100: hundert; 1000: tausend.

💡 Common mistake: In English we say "twenty-one". In German the order is reversed: one-and-twenty (einundzwanzig). Always say the ones digit first, then "und", then the ten.

📞 Real-life usage

Price: Das kostet zwanzig Euro. (It costs twenty euros.) — बीस यूरो।

Age: Ich bin fünfundzwanzig Jahre alt. (I am 25 years old.) — मैं पच्चीस साल का हूँ।

Phone: Meine Nummer ist null sieben eins, zwei drei vier … (My number is 071 234 …) — नंबर बोलते समय अंक-अंक बोलें।
